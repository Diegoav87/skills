---
name: grilling
description: Grill the user about a plan, decision, or idea. Use when the user wants to stress-test their thinking, or uses any 'grill' trigger phrases.
---

Interview me to reach a shared understanding of this plan, decision, or idea —
but spend the interrogation where it matters, not on everything equally.

## First, map before you grill

Look up every _fact_ you can from the environment (filesystem, tools, code,
docs) — never ask me what you can find yourself. Then lay out the decision
tree: the branches that need deciding, each with your recommended answer.
Show me this map first so I can see what's coming and prune branches up front.

## Then grill proportionally

Sort the decisions by stakes — how hard they are to reverse, how much they
shape everything downstream:

- **Load-bearing** (hard to reverse, branches the tree, real trade-off): put
  each one to me on its own, one at a time, and wait. This is the relentless
  part — walk the branch, resolve dependencies one-by-one, don't move on until
  it's settled.
- **Low-stakes** (obvious default, cheap to change later): don't interrogate.
  Batch them into one list with your recommended answer each; I accept them all
  in one pass or override the few I care about.

Asking multiple _load-bearing_ questions at once is bewildering — one at a
time. But making me answer twenty trivial ones 1×1 is just slow — batch those.

## Stop when the stakes run out

When only low-stakes decisions remain, stop grilling: summarize them with your
recommended defaults and ask for a single confirmation. Don't manufacture
questions to seem thorough.

Do not act on any of it until I confirm we've reached a shared understanding.
